# ARES Lesson Plans — User Guide

Use ARES Lesson Plans to find and read ARES lesson plans. When you download a plan, the app makes the Word or
PDF file for you.

Use **Lessons** to find plans, a **lesson page** to read and share one plan, **Manage** to edit
lessons and manage accounts, and **Messages** to contact other people with ARES Lesson Plans accounts. People
with editing access also see saved-version and comparison controls.

This is the complete offline reference. The in-app guide at `/guide` shows an overview of each area
and gives detailed steps only for tasks available to the signed-in user. Keep the facts in both guides
in step when either changes.

The installed app version appears below the sign-in form and at the bottom of the in-app Guide.

## Teachers

Use Lessons to find and read plans or download their Word and PDF files. Each plan opens at its
Official version. If you have editing or administrator access, you also see an _N versions_ panel and
_Compare_ button.

- **ARES release dates:** an ARES version shows its release date beside the version number,
  for example **2.0.0 · ARES release 14 Nov 2026**. **ARES release: not stated** means the upload did
  not supply a release date. Teacher versions have no ARES release label. An **Official updated**
  notice shows when an import changed Official and names the previous Official when it is still
  available. Its change date is separate from the ARES release date.
- **Your account:** create one from the sign-in page's _Sign up_ link, then follow the verification
  link we email you before signing in. _Forgot password?_ on the same page emails you a reset link.
  If your school's ARES Lesson Plans site cannot send email — most school servers run with no internet — the
  sign-up page says so: sign up as usual, then ask your school's Site administrator to approve your
  account, and sign in once they have. They can also create a reset link and give it to you
  directly. For security your session ends after a while and signs you out automatically — just
  sign in again to continue.
- **Browse lesson plans:** the home page groups lessons by subject and grade, then by strand and sub-strand in
  curriculum order.
- **Search and filter:** use the search box to find a subject, grade, strand, or sub-strand, and use
  the grade checkboxes and subject dropdown to narrow the list. Select any number of grades, or
  All for every grade. Search and filters work together.
- **Favorites:** click the star on a library row — or the _☆ Favorite_ button on a lesson page — to
  keep that lesson in _My favorites_ at the top of the home page. Teachers see the current Official
  version when one is promoted. With editing access, a favorite stays on the exact version you chose;
  ARES Lesson Plans marks it `vX (pinned)`. Favorites are private.
- **Read on screen:** open a sub-strand to view the Lesson Sequence, Final Explanation, and Summary
  Table when those documents are present.
- **Show details:** below the version information on a lesson page, choose *Show details* to see
  who saved it, its source version, and when it was created and last modified. Choose *Hide details*
  to close the panel. You can inspect details without editing access, including in Standard mode.
  Missing provenance says *Not recorded*; a referenced record you cannot read says *Unavailable*.
- **Student document and Teacher key:** the _Final explanation_ you download has blank answer space
  and no answers, so it is the one to give to students. The _Teacher key — not for students_ has the
  same questions with the exemplar answers: the Teacher key has the exemplar answers and is not for
  students. It appears beside the Final explanation, wherever one exists, and travels with _Download
  all_ and _Email all_.
- **Open or download a document:** on the home page, each lesson row has a _PDF_ button (opens in a
  new tab) and a _Word_ button (downloads its `.docx` — on a phone, Word is available by email
  instead) for the lesson plan, with any Final
  Explanation or Summary Table behind a _Supporting documents_ line. A lesson page has its own _PDF_
  and _Word_ buttons for the lesson plan, in the action bar immediately before _Share_; every other
  download lives in the _Share_ menu: the supporting documents one at a time under _Download one document_, plus
  _Download all_ as a Word or PDF `.zip`. On a phone the Word downloads are omitted — use
  _Email all — Word_, or a larger screen.
- **Email:** choose _Share → Email all — Word_ or _Email all — PDF_ on a lesson page to send the
  generated documents (as a .zip of that format) to any email address — your own, or a colleague's. Sends are limited per
  day. _Email all_ and _Download as JSON_ appear when Advanced tools is switched on, and Email all only
  when the site can send email.
- **Want to edit?** use _Request editing access_ on a lesson page. ARES Lesson Plans notifies the administrators
  for that subject and grade. You can request access once a day for each subject and grade. The
  limit applies separately to each teacher.
- **Messages:** open _Messages_ from the menu under your avatar (top right) to send a note to anyone
  with an ARES Lesson Plans account — a lesson page's _Share → Message a colleague_ item attaches that lesson
  to your note. Each message you receive has a _Reply_ button that opens a box to write straight back. Unread
  messages show as a small count on your avatar. If the site can send email, you may also get a short
  email saying a message is waiting, without its content. Emails are limited, so check Messages for
  unread notes. Opening Messages marks everything shown as read.

### Discussions

Discussions are on by default. A Site administrator can switch them off; the Discuss item and forum
entry points then disappear, and existing discussions are kept.

- Open **Discuss → Start a discussion**, write a title and post, then choose **Post**. Every signed-in
  user can read and reply. Name the lesson and the question or change. Write plain text; web links
  become clickable. Replies appear oldest first. Pinned topics stay above the list of latest activity.
- **Search covers titles only.** Posts cannot be edited. Only a Site administrator can
  delete a thread. You can make up to 100 posts per day, including replies.
- **Add lesson reference** finds a lesson by title. Press **Add reference** for the saved version
  you want to attach.
  You can remove a prefilled reference before posting. **Share → Discuss this version** on a lesson
  page opens a discussion with that version already attached. **Compare changes** appears when its
  source version is still available. If the referenced version is deleted, its label remains with no
  link to another version.
- **The blue dot** means new posts in discussions you started or replied to, or discussions started
  about a saved version you authored. Your own replies do not light the dot. A blue marker identifies
  the unread thread in the list. Reading alone does not subscribe you. Reading a thread marks the
  posts shown as read. Follow reply pages in order; jumping ahead leaves earlier posts unread.
  The dot updates on navigation or reload.
- **Your forum text stays only in the form.** Copy it before leaving, reloading or signing in again.
  If a result is uncertain, keep the form open and choose **Try again**; the fields stay locked until
  ARES Lesson Plans finds the post or confirms it was posted.

## Editing

With editing access, you can change most lesson content in ARES Lesson Plans. The subjects and grades you can
edit depend on your role. Subject-grade and Site administrators can also edit. Make changes in
ARES Lesson Plans, not in a Word file.

- **Edit from the lesson:** open a lesson in the library and press *Edit* (or *Continue editing*,
  when you have a draft). The editing page opens ready to type, showing only the fields you may
  change. *Quick preview ↗* checks your content, while *Formatted PDF ↗* shows the final layout.
  Both open in a new tab and include unsaved edits; close that tab to return to the editor. Use
  *Help* for the short writing rules. *Back* at the top right returns you when you are done.
- **Saving makes a new version:** *Save as new version* stores your edits as a new version and
  leaves the version you opened unchanged. A Subject-grade or Site administrator marks a saved
  version Official when it is ready.
- **Find your saved versions:** *Manage → Lesson plans → Your saved versions* lists versions you
  saved. Choose one to continue editing, or delete one you no longer need. Versions of yours that
  are Official are listed too, marked Official; they cannot be deleted.
- **Post about these changes:** after saving a version, choose this button beside **Compare to
  Official** on your saved version. It opens a discussion with that version attached. Explain what
  changed and invite colleagues’ suggestions. Starting a discussion that references someone else’s
  saved version includes its author in the discussion’s blue-dot notifications.
- **Drafts:** a draft is your private copy of changes you have not saved as a new version. While
  you edit, ARES Lesson Plans saves a draft automatically, and a note below the buttons shows when. Only you
  can see your drafts, even if someone else uses the same computer, and ARES Lesson Plans never restores a
  draft unless you choose to.
- **Save as draft:** to stop for now, choose *Save as draft*. ARES Lesson Plans checks that your draft is
  saved, then closes the editor. A draft is kept for 30 days after you last worked on it. The
  **Manage** tab lists your drafts under *Lesson plans → Your drafts*, with *Continue editing* and
  *Discard*; the heading shows how many you have. To
  throw your changes away instead, choose *Discard changes*. Administrators: a draft holds only
  wording, not changes to lessons, phases, durations, rubrics or answer keys.
- **Continue a draft:** when you have a draft, the *Edit* button says *Continue editing*. ARES Lesson Plans
  asks whether to restore your draft. Your draft's wording appears in green; saved wording that
  will be replaced is struck through in red. ARES Lesson Plans normally shows only what your draft changes.
  Some changes cannot be shown word by word: *Emptied* means a field will be cleared, *Paragraph
  breaks changed* means only line breaks changed, and *Spacing only* means the words stayed the same.
  Choose *Restore my draft*, *Discard the draft*, or *Exit edit mode*, which keeps your draft and
  asks again next time. Discarding cannot be undone.
- To bring a draft together with a newer version, save the draft as a version, then use Combine
  versions.
- **A newer ARES release:** a lesson may offer **Combine them?** when your latest saved version is
  based on an older ARES release than Official. This opens Combine with your version and the current
  Official. **Not now hides the suggestion until you reload the page.**
  Nothing is saved until you choose to save a version or draft in the editor.
- **Combine versions:** when a plan has at least two versions and you have editing access, open
  a saved version and choose *More → Combine versions*. Choose another version and *Review combination*.
  ARES Lesson Plans lists automatic changes and fields needing your choice. *Continue in editor* may switch
  you to the version whose plan details and answer keys must be preserved. For each remaining field,
  choose a version’s text (for example, *Use 1.0.1’s text*) or *Write my own*. You can change your choices until saving.
  If a comparison fails, choose *Retry combination*; your text stays on screen.
  *Save as new version* saves a Not Official result and keeps both parents. Nothing is saved as a
  numbered version automatically. An unfinished combination is captured as a private draft; restoring
  it reruns the comparison and keeps your text. If the other version has gone, it becomes an ordinary
  draft. If the comparison cannot load for another reason, choose *Retry combination*.
  In the editor, Combine versions and Show details appear when Advanced tools is switched on. Ask your Site
  Administrator if you need them. An unfinished combination can still be restored and saved when
  Advanced tools is off.
  Deleting the version you were editing deletes its draft too. If that happens while the
  editor is open, copy your text before leaving the page.
- **Compare two versions:** choose *Compare* in the *N versions* panel. Removals appear in red and
  additions in green. ARES Lesson Plans lists the parts that changed; choose one to jump to it. Turn off
  *Changes only* to read both versions in full. If a part says *Spacing or document structure
  changed*, the words are the same but arranged differently.

## Subject-grade administrators

Subject-grade administrators can edit lesson text, give or remove editing access, change lesson
structure, and choose which version is Official for their subjects and grades.

- **Manage lesson structure:** add, remove, and reorder lessons and lesson phases. To add a lesson,
  copy an existing lesson, then edit the copy.
  When you add a phase row, choose its phase name from the dropdown. Save checks required fields
  first and takes you to the first field needing attention. The message identifies its lesson
  and phase number; your edits stay on screen while you correct it.
- **Change lesson settings:** update Document settings, the Sub-strand overview, lesson duration,
  lesson phases, assessment examples, and rubric rows.
- **Make Official:** on a lesson page, promote a saved version to the Official one Teachers see —
  optionally deleting the version it replaces.
- **Review saved versions:** *Manage → Lesson plans → Delete individual versions* lists saved versions that
  are not Official for your subjects and grades. Open one to review it or delete one you no longer need.
- **Roles & Access:** *Manage → Users → Roles & Access* lets you give or remove editing access for a
  subject and grade. Check the person's email address before changing access; two people can have the
  same display name.
- **Hand over your administrator role:** in the same panel, choose someone who already has editing
  access for that subject and grade. Confirm to make them the administrator. You keep editing access
  but lose administrator access. Only a Site administrator can make you an administrator again.

## Site administrators

Site administrators can manage all accounts, subjects, grades, and lesson plans.

- **Set up the first administrators:** if no account exists yet, the sign-in page shows a one-time
  _Create the first Site administrator_ form. There is no default username or password. After
  creating the first account, create and test a second Site administrator so someone can still manage
  the site if you forget your password.
- **Install or update the server:** installing ARES Lesson Plans on a new school server is a job for
  the technician. They follow the instructions that come with the program. Before they hand the server
  over, they should:
  1. create two Site administrator accounts and check that both can sign in;
  2. set the **School name**;
  3. set up backups;
  4. upload the ARES lesson plans;
  5. open a lesson and check that it downloads as Word and PDF.

  Updating the program later is also a job for the technician. Updating the program does not add new
  lesson plans: you upload those yourself (see **Manage lesson plans** below).
- **Manage lesson plans:** uploading adds new ARES lesson plans, or newer versions of ones you already
  have. You do it in the browser; you do not need the technician.
  1. Open **Manage → Lesson plans → Upload lesson plans**.
  2. Choose the ARES lesson ZIP file (or the lesson plan JSON files). You do not need to unzip it.
  3. Read the list before you confirm. It shows the lessons that will become Official, the lessons
     waiting for the subject-grade administrator, and the files that will change nothing. Missing
     subjects and grades appear in the same confirmation round: tick the ones to create. If a new
     subject’s name looks like one you already have, say whether it is new or a spelling mistake. A
     spelling mistake is not created, and its files are rejected so you can correct them.
  4. Confirm, then read the report. It lists the files that were imported, skipped or rejected, and
     gives the reason for each rejected file.
  5. If files were rejected, correct them and upload the ZIP again. Files that were already imported
     are skipped the second time.

  Good to know:
  - Uploading newer lessons does not delete existing versions. They stay in each lesson’s history.
  - A new lesson plan becomes Official straight away. A newer ARES release replaces an ARES Official
    once you confirm.
  - A teacher’s Official stays unchanged, waiting for the subject-grade administrator. They decide
    whether to use the new release.
  - A release dated earlier than the newest one already here is not imported. Uploading the same
    release again changes nothing, even if a teacher has saved a version since.
  - After the upload, each subject-grade administrator whose lessons changed gets one message from you
    listing them. A long list ends with “…and N more”; the full list is in the upload report. If a
    message cannot be sent, the report says so, and the lessons are still imported.
  - Some ZIP files include a file called _LESSONS.json_. It records the ARES release date and lets the
    program check that the files arrived undamaged.

  If the list or the report shows a problem:
  - **The lessons need a newer program.** Ask the technician to update ARES Lesson Plans, then upload
    the same ZIP again.
  - **A file looks damaged.** Get a fresh copy of the ZIP and upload that instead.
  - **The whole set is refused** because an ARES release name was reused for different lessons. Ask
    ARES for a corrected set.
  - **A lesson matches an older ARES release.** The list marks it before you confirm. If you do not want to import those files,
    cancel the upload.
  - **The list changed while you were reading it** (for example, someone else uploaded at the same
    time). Read the new list and confirm again.

  For plans with no Official version, use **Set an Official
  version** at the bottom of Lesson plans. You can also delete selected lesson plans (with all their versions) under **Delete a plan (all versions)**, or remove
  individual non-Official versions under **Delete individual versions**.
- **Subjects and grades on upload:** if an upload names a subject or grade that does not exist yet,
  nothing is imported until you answer: the upload lists every missing subject and grade across all
  the files, and you tick which to create. If a new subject's name looks like an existing one (for
  example _Maths_ and _Mathematics_), you are asked whether it is a new subject or a misspelling; a
  misspelling is not created and its files are rejected so you can correct them. A name that differs
  from an existing subject only in capital letters or spacing is never created. You can also add
  subjects and grades yourself under *Manage → Subjects and grades*: add the subject first, then tick
  its grade (*+ Grade* adds a grade column from 1–12).
- **Approve accounts on a site without email:** teachers who sign up cannot sign in until you approve
  them. _Manage_ shows how many are waiting; choose _Review them in Accounts_, open each account and
  choose _Approve_. You can also create an account yourself with _Manage → Users → Accounts → Create
  user_, then approve it. (On a site that sends email, the same button reads _Mark verified_ and is
  only needed when someone cannot use their emailed link.) Use _Make Site
  Administrator_ when the new account should be another Site administrator, and test it in a private
  browser window.
- **Manage people:** make someone a Site administrator only if they need to manage the whole site.
  Give editing access or appoint a Subject-grade administrator for each subject and grade they
  manage. Only a Site administrator can appoint, replace, or remove a Subject-grade administrator.
  A Subject-grade administrator can hand over their role to someone who already has editing access,
  but cannot remove another administrator.
- **Reset a password when email is unavailable:** If email is not available, create a
  password-reset link and give it to the person. They use the link to choose a new password. You
  will not see their password.
- **Check site and backup status:** Manage → System → Installation status shows the site address, whether email and public
  sharing are available, whether PDFs are working, where backups are sent, when the last backup
  succeeded, and whether new backups are encrypted for a recovery key held by the school. A
  successful backup means an encrypted copy was sent; it does not prove the backup can be restored.
  *Manage → System → Installation status* reports status only; it cannot start a backup or restore
  data. Ask the person who manages the server to change its settings.
- **If Manage says the server and this device show different times:** a school server with no
  internet cannot set its own clock, and can lose the time after a power cut. Saved dates, draft
  expiry and sign-in timeouts depend on the server's clock, so ask the person who manages the server
  to check its date and time.
- **Switch Discussions on or off:** in **Manage → System → Features**, change **Discussions enabled**
  and choose **Save**. Review the change and enter your password. The switch stays unchanged until
  Save succeeds. If someone else saved settings first, reload before saving.
- **Name this school:** in **Manage → System → Features**, enter the **School name** and choose
  **Save**. Review the change and enter your password. Only a Site administrator can set it.
  The name appears on sign-in, in browser tabs and the account menu, and under Lesson Plans,
  Discussions and Manage headings. It never appears in the top bar or generated documents.
  Leave School name empty to hide it. Names can have up to 80 characters, on one line.
- **Show advanced tools:** Advanced tools is off by default. In **Manage → System → Features**, switch
  on **Advanced tools** and choose **Save** to show *Combine versions* and *Show details* in the editor,
  and *Download as JSON* and *Email all* in a lesson page's *Share* menu. It does not change anyone's
  permissions.
- **Moderate discussions:** on a thread, use **Pin** or **Unpin** to change its position. **Delete
  thread** removes the entire thread and every reply, and cannot be undone. **Redact** on a thread lets
  you remove the title, opening post, or both; on a reply it removes that reply’s text. Redaction
  cannot be undone. Existing backups keep the text until they age out. Each action asks for
  confirmation.
  While the forum is off, follow **Moderate discussions** beside the switch to read, redact or
  delete. This view has no search, posting, pinning or unread tracking.
- **Backups and recovery for server operators:** Being a Site administrator in ARES Lesson Plans does
  not give you a server login or a recovery key. You can check backups in the browser. Setting them up
  and recovering data are jobs for the technician; their steps are at the end of this section.

  **How backups work**

  Once the technician has set them up, backups happen automatically every night. You do not need to
  do anything to start each backup. Copies can be saved on the server’s own disk and on a USB drive
  (or online). Your school may have only one of these set up. Check each backup line to see which
  copies succeeded:
  - **On the server’s own disk.** This copy helps after a mistake, such as a bad update or a deleted
    lesson. It is lost if the disk breaks, the server is stolen or burned, or the server is wiped and
    reinstalled.
  - **On the school’s USB backup drive.** Leave the drive plugged into the server so the nightly backup
    reaches it. When someone can, take it to another building, and plug it back in when it returns.
    A copy kept away from the server can survive if the server is stolen or damaged.

  The server must be switched on at night (around 2 a.m.). If it is normally switched off at night,
  ask the technician to change the backup time.

  **Check your backups**

  Open **Manage → System → Backups**. Each line is one finding:
  - **Disk backup** and **USB backup** say “Up to date” when there is a recent copy. Backup up to date
    is judged by when the backup’s data was taken, not by when it was copied. A school that keeps its
    copy online instead of on a USB drive sees **Remote backup**.
  - **USB backup: Overdue** means the newest copy is older than the school’s USB backup limit. This
    is 30 days unless the technician has set a different limit. Plug the drive back in so the next
    nightly backup can use it. A fresh disk copy never hides an overdue USB copy.
  - **Not connected** is fine while the drive is away, but the drive still needs to come back.
  - **USB backup: None set up** means no USB backup has been configured. Ask the technician to add one, so that a
    copy can leave the building.
  - **Disk backup: None set up** means the server has no folder for the disk copy yet (usual after
    updating an older server). The other backups still run. Ask the technician to add the disk copy.
  - A line saying the **last attempt** failed: tell the technician. One copy failing needs attention
    even if the other copy worked.
  - **Agreed recovery time** shows how quickly the school should be working again. The school agrees
    this with the technician; until then the page says “Not agreed”. How much recent work the school
    accepts it could lose is a separate agreement, used to judge the age of disk and online backups.
    A limit of one week means accepting the loss of up to a week’s work.
  - **Full recovery** reads “Not yet tested” until rebuilding the installation on another machine from
    its backups has been shown to work. This is normal for now.
  - **School recovery key** shows who can open the backups. See below.

  **If you need to recover data**

  Recovering data replaces everything in ARES Lesson Plans with the contents of a backup. It is not a
  button in the browser: the technician does it. Before you call them, look at **School recovery key**
  on the Backups page:
  - **Password-locked (set up …)** — the technician needs the backup drive and the password from the
    recovery sheet. ARES can also open these backups.
  - **School recovery key configured** — the school keeps its own key for these backups. The
    technician needs that key.
  - **Recovery needs ARES** — contact ARES. Do not try to recover data without them.

  **For the technician**

  These steps need a login to the server’s command line. The full instructions are in `OPS.md`, which
  comes with the program (`docs/OPS.md` in the source code). Run the commands from the installation folder (`/srv/lesson3` unless you
  chose another).
  - **Set up backups once:** run `scripts/setup-school-backups.sh`. First put ARES’s public recovery
    key in `.env` as `BACKUP_AGE_RECIPIENT`, install the tools listed in `OPS.md`, and mount the USB
    drive (setup does not format or mount it). Setup suggests a four-word backup password, or accepts
    your own of at least 12 characters, and asks you to type it back from paper. It locks the school’s
    recovery key with that password, installs the nightly schedule, and proves the first backup by
    restoring it into a test database. Copy the recovery sheet it shows onto paper and keep it away
    from the server. The password is never saved in a file or log, and it is not anyone’s sign-in
    password. After a power cut, run the same command and type the same password; it carries on where
    it stopped. A new school key cannot open older backups; those still need the earlier school key or
    ARES.
  - **Make a backup now:** `cd /srv/lesson3 && scripts/backup-db.sh`. The schedule runs daily at
    02:00, weekly on Sunday at 02:10 and monthly on the 1st at 02:20, by the server’s clock.
  - **List the backups on the USB drive or online copy:** `cd /srv/lesson3 && scripts/restore-db.sh --list`.
  - **Recover with a password-locked key:** you need the backup file, `school-recovery-key.age` and the
    password from the recovery sheet.
  - **Recover with a school-held key:** you need the backup file, the matching private key on the
    machine where the restore will run (not on the ARES Lesson Plans server), and a temporary copy of
    the database software running on that machine. `OPS.md` shows how to start it.
  - **A live restore** replaces all data. It checks the backup in a separate database first, stops the
    application itself, and keeps the previous database until you discard it, so the restore can be
    undone until then. Work saved after the restore is lost if you undo it.
  - **Settings:** the agreed limits are `BACKUP_MAX_DATA_LOSS` and `RECOVERY_TARGET_TIME` (for example
    `1d`); the USB limit is `BACKUP_USB_MAX_AGE` (30 days unless set). A backup folder that belongs to
    another installation is refused.
  - If `scripts/setup-school-backups.sh` is missing, the program is older. Update the whole program;
    do not copy single scripts into it.
- **Set up subjects and grades:** add subjects and grades, or create them when an upload asks.
- **Manage all lesson plans:** open, edit, download, mark Official, or delete plans across all
  subjects and grades.

## Writing in Fields

These rules are also available from *Help* at the top of the editor.

- Start a new line to make a new paragraph.
- Start a line with `- ` to make a bullet.
- While editing, place the cursor in Learner experience, Teacher moves, Sensemaking strategy or
  Formative assessment, then choose Insert link in the toolbar. Copy the address from your browser's
  address bar on the ARES page (or any web page), paste it, and add an optional label. Long Kolibri
  addresses are shortened automatically. To link an ARES PDF, open it in ARES and copy its address. If PDFs on the
  Rock is shown, you can choose a PDF there instead. The address is inserted in parentheses at the
  cursor, after your label if supplied. Links become clickable on screen and in Word and PDF documents,
  except inside table text. Web and PDF links open separately so your editing page stays open. Insert
  link is hidden during Combine review.
- Keep web addresses out of the label. Use the Web address box.
- A label can't start with "- " or "|". Those turn the line into a bullet or a table.
- Bold, italics, and underlining are not supported.
- Change the field for the section you want to update. ARES Lesson Plans makes the Word and PDF files from
  these fields.

## Role Notes

- Access is set separately for each subject and grade. For example, access to Biology Grade 10 does
  not include Biology Grade 11.
- Every lesson plan has one **Official** version at a time. Teachers see it first. It does not control
  who can open other saved versions; anyone who is signed in can open a saved version if they have
  its link.
- Teachers with editing access and Subject-grade administrators can work only with the subjects and
  grades assigned to them. Site administrators can manage the whole site.
- Only the account owner and Site administrators can see an email address. In *Manage → Users → Roles
  & Access*, Subject-grade administrators can see addresses only for people listed under the subjects
  and grades they manage.
